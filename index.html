Oregon Groundfish Habitat

Python and JavaScript. NOAA trawl survey, delta-lognormal GAMs and a per-recruit model calibrated to PFMC stock assessments.

Open the app View the code

This app maps where lingcod, canary rockfish and sablefish live off Oregon, and lets you change the conditions and rules that shape those populations. The habitat models are fitted to 4,002 Oregon tows with bottom-temperature records from NOAA's West Coast Groundfish Bottom Trawl Survey (2003–2024) and predicted onto the survey's own 2-km sampling grid. A spawning-potential model calibrated to the Pacific Fishery Management Council's most recent assessment for each species sits on top. Every control starts at 2026 Oregon rules and average bottom temperatures. The tool is meant to help answer questions like:

  • If Oregon went back to a two-fish lingcod bag, how much spawning potential would that buy back?
  • How much lingcod habitat sits inside the Stonewall Bank closure, and what changes if the expansion areas close too?
  • Where does canary rockfish habitat shrink if shelf bottom water warms by 1 °C, and how sure is the model about it?
  • If low-oxygen water covers the inner shelf, how much of each species' habitat is in the way?
  • Would a 30–100 fathom closure protect the habitat that matters, or mostly push effort into open water?

On the backend: for each species, one GAM models whether a tow catches the species and a second models how much it catches when it does. Both use depth, bottom temperature, a smooth spatial surface and a year effect, and both are checked by holding out whole stretches of coastline (latitude-block AUC 0.84–0.86). A separate temperature model fills in bottom temperature for every grid cell. The closures are digitized from the regulation text: the Yelloweye Rockfish Conservation Areas in 50 CFR 660.70 and Oregon's five marine reserves. Life history and stock status come from the assessments. The handful of inputs that aren't published (sport share of fishing mortality, encounters per trip, release mortality) are labelled in the app as assumptions and can be changed there.

Kit Fox Oregon Outlook

R and JavaScript. iNaturalist records, WorldClim and CMIP6 climate, an effort-corrected GAM and lasso ensemble, and a habitat-network viability test.

Open the app View the code

Kit foxes are listed as threatened under Oregon's Endangered Species Act, and they reach the northern edge of their range in the desert of Harney and Malheur counties. This project asks a reintroduction question: is there a connected block of habitat large enough to hold a viable population, now and as the climate changes? The habitat model is trained on cleaned, research-grade iNaturalist records from across the species' range outside Oregon. Oregon's own records are held out as an independent check. The model is then projected onto Oregon for 1970–2000 and four CMIP6 periods to 2100.

On the backend: GAM and lasso point-process models (infinitely weighted logistic regression) are averaged. Predictors are climate, soils (SoilGrids), land cover (ESA WorldCover) and terrain ruggedness. Population density and travel time to towns are fitted as an observer-effort term, then held constant when predicting. Spatial block cross-validation uses 250 km blocks. Suitable cells within dispersal distance join into networks; a network's carrying capacity is its area times a Great Basin density estimate, and it counts as viable once that capacity reaches the minimum viable population.
